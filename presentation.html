Current Roles and Affiliations

Lead AI Engineer at Fastweb (Rome, Italy):

Since April 2025, Berardino Barile has been Lead AI Engineer at Fastweb in Rome. He leads large language model (LLM) and agentic automation initiatives, designing, building and deploying LLM and multi-agent systems that automate complex, knowledge-intensive workflows end to end, and taking solutions from research prototypes to production-grade services.

His current work spans vision-language retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for document intelligence, steering and fine-tuning of large language models (activation steering, instruction tuning, LoRA/PEFT), and a real-time agentic voice assistant that orchestrates tool use, function calling, and speech-to-text and text-to-speech to carry out operational tasks. His work bridges academic research and real-world AI applications, driven by a passion for solving real-world problems using AI.

Previous Professional Experience

Research Scientist at McGill University & Concordia University, within the Mila AI Institute, Montréal, Canada (Mar 2023 – Apr 2025): Led AI research projects in computer vision in collaboration with the Probabilistic Vision Group at McGill University (Centre for Intelligent Machines, supervised by Prof. Tal Arbel), the IMPACT Lab at Concordia University and Mila – Quebec’s AI Institute. Used generative AI methods and causal machine learning for zero-shot prediction and causal reasoning on longitudinal disease evolution, embedding tabular and image data to simulate counterfactual scenarios that support the management of risks and side effects of new experimental drugs. He also worked as a teaching assistant at McGill (probability theory, Bayesian inference and deep generative models) and as a research consultant for La Sapienza University of Rome on credit risk and the survival of small and medium Italian enterprises.

Senior Data Scientist at Verti S.p.A. (Mapfre Group), Milan, Italy (Jul 2022 – Mar 2023): Led machine learning initiatives in insurance: models for optimal pricing, churn prediction and customer retention, plus constrained optimization (linear programming and metaheuristics) that balanced risk and profitability. Production pipelines delivered a 2% increase in revenue and a 5% reduction in customer churn, and automated AWS SageMaker pipelines cut deployment time by 30%. He also collaborated with Sapienza University of Rome and the Italian workers’ compensation authority (INAIL) on modeling workplace accidents and business failure with causal inference and predictive modeling.

Big Data Scientist at Isiway S.r.l., Rome, Italy (Nov 2017 – May 2019): Implemented machine learning solutions for big data consulting projects: deep learning models for predicting human trajectories, customer behavior analysis (for example mouse tracking) and recommendation systems that raised retention by 20%, and deep learning NLP for sentiment analysis of web-scraped text (word embeddings, RNN/LSTM and Transformers) that improved prediction accuracy by 25%.

Data Analyst at Invitalia S.p.A. (National Agency for Investment and Economic Development), Rome, Italy (Apr 2014 – Nov 2017): Provided statistical analysis and policy evaluation research to support public decision-making. Evaluated the solidity and solvability of listed companies for investment risk assessment and portfolio management, and developed econometric models for public policy evaluation: survival analysis of small and medium enterprises, non-parametric clustering and density estimation, and probabilistic matching to simulate counterfactual scenarios.

Data Analyst (Intern) at Johnson & Johnson Medical (MedTech), Italy (2013–2014): Worked on healthcare data analytics, developing statistical models and mathematical optimization, as his first post-graduate experience following his M.Sc.

Education and Academic Background

Double PhD in Computer Science, KU Leuven (Belgium) & Université Claude Bernard Lyon 1 (France), 2019–2022: Marie Skłodowska-Curie Action PhD fellowship, supervised by Sabine Van Huffel and Dominique Sappey-Marinier. Thesis: “Machine Learning Methods for Multiple Sclerosis Classification and Prediction Using MRI Brain Connectivity”.

M.Sc. in Statistics, La Sapienza University of Rome (Italy), 2011–2013: 110/110 cum laude. Thesis: “Short and Long Structural Effects of the International Bank System: A Structural-VAR Approach”, supervised by Bernardo Maggi.

B.Sc. in Statistics, La Sapienza University of Rome (Italy), 2008–2011: 110/110 cum laude. Thesis: “Purchase Power Parity (PPP) and Equilibrium Exchange Rate in the Financial Market”, supervised by Bernardo Maggi.

His multilingual ability (Italian native, English advanced, French C2 with a TEF certificate, Spanish beginner) and international study/work experience in Italy, France, Belgium, and Canada highlight a global academic perspective.
